About epilepsy

A condition most people have heard of, and few people recognise.

Globally, 1 in 26 people have epilepsy. Although it is one of the earliest documented neurological conditions, there is still stigma surrounding epilepsy. Our organization believes that such stigma stems around a lack of awareness on how to help such individuals when they are seizing. Thus, it is our mission to provide resources that allow community members to support and understand those with epilepsy. Because managing seizures shouldn’t be a problem that individuals suffer from alone, it should be a problem that we as a society work together to help. We hope you find our resources useful in your communities!! - The Seizure Space Team

What is Epilepsy?

Epilepsy is a neurological condition where there are abnormal bursts of electrical activity in the brain, leading to frequent seizures. In television and the media, seizures are often portrayed as violent shaking and aggressive movements, however every patient’s seizure is different. There are over 40 specific types of epilepsy and around 30 types of epileptic seizures. As a community it is crucial to understand that seizures and epilepsy can come in many shapes and sizes and that we need to provide different support for people with different types of epilepsy. According to the International League Against Epilepsy (ILAE), epilepsy can be separated into 4 types: focal, generalized, Combined generalized and focal, and unknown onset. Focal seizures refer to seizures that originate from abnormal electrical activity in only one localized area of the brain. Generalized seizures refer to seizures that originate from both hemispheres or a large area of the brain. Combined Generalized and Focal epilepsy is where patients can get both focal and generalized seizures. With unknown onset seizures, the seizures are either focal or generalized but it’s not possible to determine which part of the brain the seizure originates from. Sometimes this can be determined later on once doctors have more information on the seizure patterns of the patient.

Seizure and Epilepsy Diversity

Here at The Seizure Space we prioritize acknowledging the diversity of seizures and epilepsy. Epilepsy can look very different in different people and it is important that all of their seizures are understood and supported. Our tools and resources aim to address this diversity and provide bystanders and communities with the knowledge to do this.

What our Organization does

Here are all the resources we provide to help the support of those with epilepsy:

NFC tags

Keychain tags with both a Near Field Communication (NFC) chip and a QR code. Used by individuals with epilepsy, the QR code and NFC both lead to a personalized webpage with information about how to help the individual during a seizure. It can be scanned by bystanders, allowing them to provide appropriate and immediate support. The webpage is secure and individuals have full control of what information they choose to share.

Online Course

Sign up for our online course on seizure first aid! The course teaches you the basics of seizures and what epilepsy is before diving into how to help someone during a seizure.

First Aid Kit

Instructions on how to create an epilepsy first aid kit for regular use by those with generalized tonic clonic seizures. The kit allows you to streamline the process of helping someone with generalized tonic clonic seizures.

Awareness Information

Regular instagram posts on common epilepsy myths and facts. Frequent discussion on what can be done to improve the experience of those with epilepsy and what communities can do to support them.

This page is general information, not medical advice. Epilepsy varies enormously between individuals. For guidance about your own situation, or that of someone you care for, speak to a doctor or a specialist epilepsy nurse.
